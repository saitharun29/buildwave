# AI + Learning Notes

How I used AI on this challenge — and, just as important, where I overrode it. Format: **what I asked → what AI suggested → what I changed and why.**

---

### 1. The channel strategy

**I asked:** "How do I get 500 final-year engineering students to a free workshop with ₹2,000 in 7 days?"

**AI suggested:** A broad mix — Instagram ads, Google ads, Facebook groups, email blasts, influencer shout-outs, posters, and more. About a dozen channels.

**What I changed:** Cut it to **three**, in priority order (college WhatsApp groups → clubs/ambassadors → LinkedIn/email), and dropped paid ads entirely. The brief explicitly said *don't give 20 ideas — prioritise.* More importantly, ₹2,000 buys ~10–15 ad clicks, so paid acquisition can't reach 500. The budget *forces* an organic, referral-led plan. I reframed the ₹2,000 as a **referral reward pool**, not ad spend.

---

### 2. The thing to build

**I asked:** "What one asset should I build to support this plan?"

**AI suggested:** A landing page with a hero, benefits, and a registration form.

**What I changed:** Rejected it. The brief literally says *"Everyone might build a landing page."* A form also only *collects* registrations — it doesn't *generate* them. I built a **referral engine with a live leaderboard** instead, so the asset *is* the growth mechanism. A landing page is passive; a referral loop compounds, which is the only way the math reaches 500 on this budget.

---

### 3. The conversion hook

**I asked:** "How do I make students actually click register instead of scrolling past?"

**AI suggested:** A strong headline plus a bullet list of workshop benefits ("learn AI", "hands-on", "certificate").

**What I changed:** Benefit bullets are generic and forgettable. I built an **interactive idea generator** in the hero instead: the student picks their interest and instantly sees *the specific project they'll walk out with* ("an AI resume reviewer", "a phishing detector"). Showing a personalised, concrete outcome is far more persuasive than listing abstract benefits — especially for placement-anxious students who want a resume line.

---

### 4. The copy

**I asked:** AI to draft the WhatsApp share message and page copy.

**AI suggested:** Polished, slightly corporate marketing copy ("Unlock your potential with cutting-edge AI!").

**What I changed:** Rewrote it in a student's own voice ("I just grabbed a seat… you walk out with a real project for your résumé"). The message travels through *peer* WhatsApp groups, so it has to sound like a friend, not a brand. I also kept all interface copy plain and action-led ("Reserve my seat", "Copy", "Share on WhatsApp") rather than clever.

---

## What I learned

- **Constraints are the brief.** The ₹2,000 wasn't a detail — it was the whole puzzle. Spotting that it rules out paid ads is what pointed to the referral strategy.
- **AI is a fast first-drafter, not the decision-maker.** Every strong choice here came from *rejecting* the obvious AI default (ads, landing page, benefit bullets) against the specific constraints and audience.
- **Build the mechanism, not the brochure.** The most defensible asset is the one that *is* the growth loop, not one that merely describes the offer.
